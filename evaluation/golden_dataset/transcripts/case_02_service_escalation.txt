Meridian Motors — Recall & Service Notification Experience Escalation
Attendees: Anika (Connected Services PM), Elena (Compliance), Devon (Director Eng),
           Marcus (Companion App Lead), Priya (VP Owner Experience)
Date: May 9

Anika opened — said proactive recall and service notification complaints
are spiking in the in-app feedback. Owners tell us they didn't know there
was an open recall on their vehicle, or worse, they got a notification
and then drove to the dealer only to find out the alert had been pushed
to the wrong driver on a shared vehicle account. Two dealer service
managers in the Phoenix cluster filed formal complaints last week.

------

Problem 1: Recall-ready notifications are unreliable

Today the in-app notification fires only when the owner opens the
companion app and the home screen polls the RecallHub service. If they
never open the app, they never know the recall or service action is
ready. We are leaving safety actions unactioned — and worse, owners are
hearing about open recalls from the news instead of from us.

Anika's ask: a proactive push notification when a recall or service
action is open for the vehicle. Marcus said the mobile push
infrastructure is in place — this is a backend trigger problem off the
RecallHub event, not a client problem.

Anika: "And we want the notification text to include the specific recall
code so the owner recognizes it. Like 'Recall 24V-118 brake actuator
on your vehicle.'"

Elena cut in immediately — that is not OK under GDPR/CCPA. Surfacing a
specific defect or recall code in a push notification requires explicit
owner consent stored on the vehicle record, AND it can only go to the
owner's verified contact method, never the shared fleet account default.
We've been bitten by this before.

Anika: "Right, sorry. We can do a generic 'A service action is available
for your vehicle' and let them open the app to see details.
Detail-on-tap, not detail-in-banner."

Elena: "That's fine. Anything that does show the recall code needs
consent plus verified delivery plus a 7-year audit log entry."

Devon: "Let's design it both ways and flag the consent version as a
follow-up. Phase 1 ships generic, phase 2 adds consent for the
recall-code surface."

------

Problem 2: The shared-vehicle-account problem

Today multiple drivers can be on a single vehicle account through VIS.
RecallHub knows which action belongs to which VIN and owner of record,
but the notification system fires to the account, not the verified owner.
That is why the Phoenix dealer saw a notification go to a co-driver about
the primary owner's recall.

Marcus: "We need owner-scoped notification delivery. The push goes only
to the device that's logged in as that specific owner identity through
VIS."

Elena: "Yes, this is the verified contact method requirement applied to
push. It's not optional."

Devon assigned this to Marcus and Anika together with Elena as the
compliance reviewer.

------

Problem 3: SMS fallback

Anika brought up SMS — some owners are older and don't use the app.
Could we send the service-action notice by SMS too?

Marcus paused. "Isn't AD-176 already for that?" Devon checked the
backlog — yes, AD-176 is the warranty/service push-notification-when-
parts-are-ordered ticket and already carries the SMS fallback scope.
It's in backlog status. Anika: "Let's just push AD-176 up the priority
list then. No new story needed."

------

Things we explicitly said NO to this round:
- Voice-call reminders (Anika asked, Devon said no — too expensive
  and not requested by owners).
- Adding recall notifications to the in-vehicle infotainment lock screen
  — out of scope for this round.

------

Next steps:
Anika owns the proactive notification spec. Marcus owns the owner-scoped
delivery work. Elena will draft the GDPR consent review checklist.
AD-176 stays as the SMS workstream.
