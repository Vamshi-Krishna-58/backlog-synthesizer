Meridian Motors — Service & Recall Notification Experience Escalation
Attendees: Nalini (Warranty & Service PM), Yusuf (Compliance), Ravi (Director Vehicle Software),
           Kenji (Mobile App Lead), Sarah (VP Connected Experience)
Date: May 9

Nalini opened — said service and recall complaints are spiking in the
companion app feedback. Owners tell us they didn't know their vehicle
had an open recall, or worse, they got a notification and then drove to
the dealer only to find out the alert had pushed the wrong vehicle's
service item to a shared fleet account. Two dealer groups in the
Phoenix region filed formal complaints last week.

------

Problem 1: Recall-ready notifications are unreliable

Today the in-app notification fires only when the owner opens the
companion app and the home screen polls RecallHub. If they never open
the app, they never learn there's an open recall. We are leaving real
safety actions undone — and worse, owners are servicing at independent
shops because they got a postcard but no Meridian alert.

Nalini's ask: a proactive push notification when a recall or due
service is ready to schedule. Kenji said the mobile push infrastructure
is in place — this is a backend trigger problem off the RecallHub event
stream, not a client problem.

Nalini: "And we want the notification text to include the specific
recall so the owner recognizes it. Like 'Recall 24V-318: brake booster
inspection is ready to schedule at Camelback Meridian.'"

Yusuf cut in immediately — that is not OK. Under GDPR/CCPA and our
Section 3 rules, surfacing a specific defect code in a push requires
explicit owner consent stored on the vehicle record, AND it can only go
to the owner's *verified* contact method, never the shared fleet account
default. We've been bitten by this before.

Nalini: "Right, sorry. We can do a generic 'You have an open recall —
open the app for details' and let them tap through. Detail-on-tap, not
detail-in-banner."

Yusuf: "That's fine. Anything that does show the defect code needs
opt-in plus verified delivery plus an audit log entry retained seven
years."

Ravi: "Let's design it both ways and flag the opt-in version as a
follow-up. Phase 1 ships generic, phase 2 adds opt-in for the recall
detail surface."

------

Problem 2: The shared fleet-account problem

Today multiple drivers and a fleet manager can be on a single vehicle
account. RecallHub knows which action belongs to which VIN, but the
notification system fires to the account, not the verified owner. That
is why the Phoenix dealer saw a recall alert go to a spouse about a
vehicle assigned to the other driver.

Kenji: "We need owner-scoped notification delivery. The push goes only
to the device logged in as that specific owner identity through VIS,
bound to the VIN."

Yusuf: "Yes — this is the verified contact method requirement applied to
push. It's not optional."

Ravi assigned this to Kenji and Nalini together with Yusuf as the
compliance reviewer.

------

Problem 3: SMS fallback

Nalini brought up SMS — many owners are over 65 and don't use the app.
Could we send the recall-ready notice by SMS too?

Kenji paused. "Isn't AD-176 already for that?" Ravi checked the backlog
— yes, AD-176 is the service-reminder SMS fallback. It's in backlog
status. Nalini: "Let's just push AD-176 up the priority list then. No
new story needed."

------

Things we explicitly said NO to this round:
- Automated voice-call reminders (Nalini asked, Ravi said no — too
  expensive and not requested by owners).
- Surfacing recall alerts on the in-cluster head unit while driving —
  out of scope for this round and a driver-distraction concern.

------

Next steps:
Nalini owns the proactive notification spec. Kenji owns the owner-scoped
delivery work. Yusuf will draft the consent/audit review checklist.
AD-176 stays as the SMS workstream.
